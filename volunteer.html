PHOTO 02 / THE VOLUNTEER'S JOURNEY

Turning Experiences Into Stories

After nearly three years of volunteering at the hospital, I realized how many stories I had carried home with me.

I met children who were afraid to smile. I helped international families who struggled to explain their concerns in a foreign language. I also met parents who tried to stay strong in front of their children, even when they were worried themselves.

Not every day was memorable. Some days were spent organizing insurance documents or guiding patients through the hospital. But even during those ordinary moments, I learned something new about people.

I began to notice the small things. A parent holding a child's hand a little tighter. A patient looking relieved after finally understanding the doctor's instructions. A quiet thank-you at the end of a long day.

I did not want these memories to disappear.

So I began writing The Volunteer's Journey, a book based on the people I met and the lessons I learned during my time at the hospital. I spent time looking back at my experiences and putting those memories into words. Seeing the finished book in my hands was one of my proudest moments.

Writing this book also reminded me why I want to become a journalist. I have always been interested in telling stories, but volunteering taught me that the most meaningful stories are not always the loudest ones.

Sometimes, they belong to people who are too afraid to speak or simply need someone willing to listen.

I hope to carry that lesson with me, both as a volunteer and as a future journalist.
