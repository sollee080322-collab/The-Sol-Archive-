Hospital Volunteering

Nearly three years of learning that care begins with listening, noticing and staying a little longer.

SECTION 01 / INTERNATIONAL HEALTHCARE

Beyond Language

PHOTO 01 / AT THE HOSPITAL
PHOTO 02 / BEHIND THE DESK

When I first began volunteering at CHA Hospital, I thought helping patients meant showing them where to go or answering simple questions.

At the International Health Clinic, I soon learned that even a routine hospital visit could feel overwhelming to someone unfamiliar with the language and healthcare system.

I helped international patients with registration, insurance paperwork and directions to their appointments. Sometimes I contacted translators or explained the next steps to families who were unsure what to do.

One day, I met an Indian family who had come to the hospital with their sick child. They were trying to navigate an unfamiliar system while worrying about their child's health.

I stayed with them to help communicate with the hospital staff and understand the paperwork. Even after my scheduled volunteering hours ended, I wanted to make sure they knew what to do next.

Later, I received a message expressing their gratitude. What stayed with me was not the message itself, but the realization that a small amount of extra time could make someone feel less alone.

SECTION 02 / WRITING & REFLECTION

The Volunteer's Journey

PHOTO 03 / THE BOOK
PHOTO 04 / THE JOURNEY

After spending years at the hospital, I began writing down the moments I did not want to forget. Some were ordinary conversations. Others were experiences that stayed with me long after I returned home.

I wrote about the people I met, the situations I struggled to understand and the lessons I learned from being present for others.

Eventually, those reflections became a published book, The Volunteer's Journey.

Writing the book gave me a chance to revisit experiences I had once considered small. I began to see how each interaction had changed the way I understood responsibility and empathy.

It also brought together two things I care deeply about: helping people and telling stories. I wanted to preserve the voices and moments that might otherwise be forgotten.

"Sometimes, the most meaningful thing we can offer someone is our time."
